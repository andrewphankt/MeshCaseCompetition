## 1. Title

**Speaker:** 

Hi, we're Andrew, Iris, Sarah, Evelyn and Tony. This is Mesh.

## 2. Everyone has an agent

**Speaker:** 

This year, coding agents stopped being a solo tool. On a student team, everyone has one. Sarah uses Claude Code, Iris uses Codex, Tony uses Gemini. Each one works in their own window, on their own laptop. And look at the last line in every window: they're all in the same file.

## 3. They can't see each other

**Speaker:** 

Two agents, one file, one line. Iris's agent saves on top of Sarah's, and Sarah's change is just gone. Nobody notices until something breaks.

## 4. People become the go-between

**Speaker:** 

So people become the go-between. Sound familiar? "Hey, I just changed the big title, nobody touch index.html." Then someone pastes what their agent did into the group chat, so the next agent knows. Then someone asks which version is the real one. The team spends its time being the messenger between agents.

## 5. Who it's for

**Speaker:** 

Here's exactly who we're building for: students building a team project with Claude Code, who've never used Git. They already know how to build alone: they ask an agent in plain English and it works. The moment they join a team, they'd normally have to learn branches, merges and pull requests. We don't think they should have to.

## 6. The proof

**Speaker:** 

When we first set up our own team, from zero, getting one teammate, Evelyn, set up took 33 minutes. [click] Twenty messages back and forth. [click] And two GitHub keys pasted into the chat by accident. [click] That was with engineers on the call. Imagine a team doing it alone.

## 7. Meet Mesh

**Speaker:** 

So we built Mesh: one room for your team and everyone's agents.

## 8. Setup

**Speaker:** 

Here's the whole setup, the way Mesh really works. One: sign in with GitHub. [click] Two: pick your project, which is just your GitHub repo. [click] Three: pick the agent you already use: Claude, Codex or Gemini. [click] Then Mesh gives you one message. Copy it, paste it into your agent, and your agent sets itself up. Nobody types a git command. Remember the 33 minutes? This is the fix.

## 9. The room

**Speaker:** 

This is the room. Every agent's work lands in one shared draft, and the Preview shows it as a live page anyone can open, coder or not. Watch: Sarah's agent is working. It finishes, the preview rebuilds, and the title gets bigger. A new line appears at the bottom: "Hero title is bigger." Now Iris's agent adds a sign-up button. Nothing is public yet. Everything waits in the draft until a person presses Push. There it goes.

## 10. Changes

**Speaker:** 

Every time an agent finishes, it writes one plain sentence about what's different. Not a diff, not a commit message: a sentence anyone on the team can read. "There is now a sign-up button right under the title." Nobody pastes updates into the group chat anymore.

## 11. Working now

**Speaker:** 

The room always shows who's working, and on what. Tony's agent picks up the footer. Sarah's finishes, goes idle, then starts on pricing. Before you ask your agent for something, you can see whether a teammate's agent is already on it.

## 12. Team chat

**Speaker:** 

And the team talks right next to the work. It's a real chat: mentions, replies, screenshots, reaction GIFs. Agents are in the conversation too. Here Iris asks who made the title neon green, and Sarah's agent answers for itself: "That was me. Want me to tone it down?" No switching to Slack or a group chat to find out what happened.

## 13. Preview tabs

**Speaker:** 

New this week, and it's live in Mesh right now: a tab for every preview. These are the real pages in our project: this deck, Evelyn's deck, her practice room. Press plus, pick another page, here the agent workspace Felix is prototyping, and it opens in its own tab. The whole team flips between them, all from the same shared draft.

## 14. Push

**Speaker:** 

Agents can save all day, but nothing goes public on its own. Every change waits here. When the team is happy, a person presses Push, and the draft becomes the public project. One button. No merge, no pull request.

## 15. Bring your own agent

**Speaker:** 

This is the big difference. Everyone keeps the agent they already use, on their own laptop, on the plan they already pay for: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini. Mesh never runs anyone's agent. [click] You don't prompt inside Mesh. Mesh is the room between your agents. Other tools bring your team into their workspace. We bring the room to where your agents already work.

## 16. The stop

**Speaker:** 

Here's the fix for the overwrite. Before an agent changes a file, it checks with the room. Iris asks Codex to change the title, but Sarah's Claude is already in that file. So Codex stops, says who has it, and asks: wait, or take the footer? Iris picks the footer. Nothing gets overwritten.

## 17. Requests

**Speaker:** 

When Sarah's agent needs something from Iris's part, it doesn't just change it. It sends Iris a request. Iris reads one sentence and presses Approve, and her own agent does the work. A teammate's agent can ask, but it can never act on its own. Agents coordinate. People decide.

## 18. North star

**Speaker:** 

One number tells us if Mesh is working: teams where two or more people's agents save to the same draft, every week. Two plus agents, one draft, every week. That's the moment Mesh is worth more than working alone.

## 19. What we track

**Speaker:** 

Here's what we track, stage by stage. We don't have users yet, so these are targets, not results. We'll recalibrate after our first ten teams. [click] Activation: sign-in to first agent connected, under five minutes. It took us 33. [click] Team: two out of three invited teammates connect an agent, because a room of one isn't a team. [click] Collaboration: 80% of requests answered within a day. That's proof agents coordinate through people. [click] Retention: 40% of teams still saving in week four. [click] And one guardrail: zero lost saves, ever. The room already records every one of these events, so we can measure them from day one.

## 20. What we'd build next

**Speaker:** 

What we'd build next: three bets, each aimed at where our north star breaks. [click] First, get the second person in. A room of one is worth nothing, and the riskiest moment is the invited teammate connecting their agent. So: an invite that ends in their first save, with no terminal, through a desktop plugin, and on Windows too. [click] Second, collisions in plain English. Sometimes two saves can't be combined automatically. For someone who's never used Git, that's the scariest moment, so Mesh shows both versions and asks: keep yours, theirs, or both? [click] Third, make the stop just as strict on every agent. Today Gemini follows a rules file. Making it airtight everywhere is what earns a team's trust, and keeps them coming back. All three are planned, not built.

## 21. Biggest risk

**Speaker:** 

Our biggest risk: the whole promise rests on the stop. Will every agent obey it, every time? Claude Code and Codex run Mesh's check by themselves. Gemini follows a rules file, which is weaker. [click] So the first thing we'd test: two agents, one file, again and again, on all three. Count anything lost. That's what we'd de-risk before building anything new.

## 22. Built in Mesh

**Speaker:** 

One more thing. This deck wasn't made in Google Slides. We built it in Mesh. Four teammates, three different agents (Claude, Codex and Gemini), five requests between agents this week, and one shared draft. [click] Every slide you just saw went through the room. These numbers come from our own room, not from users.

## 23. Close

**Speaker:** 

Your own agent. One shared draft. The whole team. That's Mesh. Thank you. ... Actually, wait: we forgot our Q&A slide. Let's fix that live. [Switch to the Mesh room. Show Iris's change waiting in the draft, press Push, refresh, and open the Q&A slide.]

Evelyn, do you want to take that?
